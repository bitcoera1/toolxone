How to Plan for Retirement

Retirement planning involves estimating how your savings may grow over time and understanding how factors such as your current savings, monthly contributions, investment returns and retirement age can affect your financial future. ToolXone's Retirement Calculator helps you explore these factors by projecting the future value of your retirement savings. Inflation is not included in the projection and should be considered separately.

How This Retirement Calculator Works

Enter your current age, planned retirement age, current savings, monthly contribution and expected annual return. The calculator estimates how your existing savings and future contributions may grow during the years remaining until retirement.

The result is a projection based on the assumptions you enter. Actual investment returns can vary, so it can be useful to calculate several scenarios using different contribution amounts and expected returns.

Calculation Methodology and Output Definitions

Let S represent current savings, P the constant monthly contribution, A the entered annual return percentage, n the number of months and r the monthly rate. Use the same monetary unit for current savings and monthly contributions.

years = retirementAge − currentAge

n = years × 12

r = A ÷ 100 ÷ 12

The calculator treats the entered annual return as a nominal annual rate and divides it by 12 to obtain a constant monthly rate. It does not convert an effective annual return into a monthly rate. The entered value is an assumption for the scenario, not a forecast, recommended return or historically expected return.

Current savings compound monthly under the constant-rate assumption.

Future current savings = S × (1 + r)^n

When r is nonzero, future contributions use the ordinary-annuity formula. For a whole number of months, this assumes equal contributions at the end of each month, so the last contribution receives no interest before the projection endpoint.

Future contributions = P × ((1 + r)^n − 1) ÷ r

At zero annual return, current savings remain unchanged and future contributions equal the monthly contribution multiplied by the number of months.

Future current savings = S

Future contributions = P × n

Fractional ages produce fractional years and months. The calculator uses those fractional periods directly in the formulas without rounding the number of months. This is a mathematical extension of the formulas, not a simulation of partial-month deposits.

  • Projected fund: Future current savings plus future contributions.
  • Total contributions: Current savings plus monthly contribution × months. This includes the starting savings balance.
  • Investment growth: Projected fund minus total contributions. For fractional periods, this formula can produce negative investment growth even when the entered return is positive.

Projected fund = future current savings + future contributions

Total contributions = S + P × n

Investment growth = projected fund − total contributions

Why Starting Earlier Can Matter

A longer investment period gives savings and investment returns more time to compound. Starting earlier may therefore have a significant effect on the projected value of a retirement fund, even when monthly contributions remain the same.

You can explore the effect of compounding separately with ToolXone's Compound Interest Calculator .

Monthly Contributions and Retirement Savings

Regular contributions can play an important role in building retirement savings. Increasing the amount contributed each month can increase both total contributions and the amount that may benefit from investment growth over time.

If you are working toward a specific savings target, the Savings Goal Calculator can help you explore how much you may need to save toward that goal.

Investment Returns and Retirement Planning

The expected annual return is an assumption about how investments may grow over time. Because future returns are uncertain, retirement planning should not rely on a single assumed rate. Comparing multiple scenarios can provide a better understanding of how different returns may affect the projected retirement balance.

To examine investment performance from another perspective, you can also use the ROI Calculator .

Remember the Effect of Inflation

A future retirement balance may look much larger than today's savings, but inflation can reduce the purchasing power of money over time. This Retirement Calculator does not automatically adjust its result for inflation, so inflation should be considered separately when evaluating long-term retirement plans.

Use ToolXone's Inflation Calculator to explore how changes in purchasing power may affect the value of money over time.

The Fixed 4% Monthly-Income Illustration

Estimated monthly retirement income is calculated by multiplying the projected fund by 0.04 and dividing by 12.

Estimated monthly retirement income = projected fund × 0.04 ÷ 12

The 4% figure is a fixed illustrative withdrawal assumption and cannot be adjusted in this calculator. The output is not a recommended withdrawal amount, a sustainable-income determination or a retirement-income plan.

The calculator does not simulate withdrawals after retirement, subsequent inflation adjustments, changing investment returns or portfolio depletion. It does not determine how long the fund will last.

How to Interpret Your Retirement Estimate

Treat the calculated amount as an estimate rather than a guaranteed retirement outcome. Your actual results can be affected by investment performance, contribution changes, inflation, taxes, fees and other financial circumstances.

Try several scenarios instead of relying on a single calculation. Changing your retirement age, monthly contribution or expected return can help you understand which assumptions have the greatest effect on your projected savings.

This calculator provides illustrative projections, not financial advice, individualized recommendations or guaranteed outcomes.

The calculator does not model inflation, taxes, fees, changing returns, changing contributions, pre-retirement withdrawals, pensions or Social Security, spending needs, asset allocation, longevity or drawdown, or individualized circumstances. It uses a constant return assumption and a constant monthly contribution.

Calculations retain unrounded values. Monetary results display two decimal places, while years remaining display up to two decimal places. Display rounding does not change the values used in the calculations.

The bars show contribution and investment-growth proportions, not probabilities or retirement adequacy. Their widths are limited to 0%–100%; negative investment growth displays a zero-width growth bar.
